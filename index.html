🌍 The Denisovan Connection

🦴 3.6% Denisovan DNA - What Does This Mean?

Denisovans were an extinct human species who lived in Asia. Our elevated Denisovan percentage suggests our maternal ancestors spent significant time in Southeast Asia or the eastern Himalayan corridor, where Denisovan admixture was higher.

Important context: Denisovan percentages vary significantly depending on testing methodology. Our 3.6% from NatGeo is notably higher than typical mainland Asian averages (0.1-0.5%). The highest confirmed Denisovan ancestry (4-6%) is found in Melanesian and some Filipino populations. If accurate, our percentage would be unusually high for mainland Asia.

🏛️ The Kirat Connection: Were Our Ancestors Among Kathmandu's Earliest Rulers?

This section draws from an interview with Bhogiraj Chamling, a PhD Fellow in Anthropology at Tribhuvan University and expert in Mundhum (Kirat oral tradition), Kirat History, and Archaeology.

📜 The Kirat Dynasty: First Documented Rulers

According to historical chronicles (Vamsavalis) and oral traditions, the Kirats were the first documented rulers of Kathmandu Valley, governing for approximately 1,200 years before being displaced by the Licchavi dynasty around 2,000 years ago. But what happened to the Kirat people after they lost power?

🔥 The Chyasal Evidence

In Patan, there's a place called Chyasal (च्यासल) — which means "800" in Newari. According to Chamling:

"This place marks where 800 Kirat warriors were killed and buried after their defeat. The site still has memorial structures preserved to this day."

This physical evidence in our home region of Patan directly connects to the Kirat history.

✨ Our Family's Direct Connection

Our maternal grandmother — Maaich Maa — is from Chyasal. This means our maternal line — the same line carrying the M9 haplogroup that traces back through the Eastern Himalayan corridor — comes directly from the site commemorating the fallen Kirat warriors.

This isn't just a historical connection — it's a family connection. The very place where Kirat history ended in Kathmandu is where our maternal ancestry continued.

👥 Three Types of Kirats

Chamling explains that after the Licchavi conquest, the Kirat population split into three groups:

1. बास्ति किराति (Basti Kirat) — "Those Who Stayed"

Kirats who remained in Kathmandu Valley and eventually became part of the Newar community. They are identified today as:

  • Jyapu (farming caste)
  • Maharjan ← Our family's caste
  • Dangol
  • Other agricultural Newar groups

2. लास्ति किराति (Lasti Kirat) — "First Wave"

Those who left Kathmandu in the first wave of displacement, migrating eastward through Banepa and Dolakha.

3. गास्ति किराति (Gasti Kirat) — "Last Wave"

The final group who left after the last king's defeat. The Vamsavalis record this king as "Gasti" — matching the oral tradition name.

💡 Key Insight: Maharjan = Kirat Descendants

According to Chamling, the Maharjan caste — our family's caste — represents Kirats who stayed in Kathmandu and became integrated into Newar society. He states:

"The Jyapu community within Newars — including Maharjan and Dangol — these are the Kirat descendants who remained. They are the oldest continuous inhabitants of Kathmandu Valley. Some Dangol families in Chyasal still identify as Kirat even today."

🧬 Genetic Evidence Chamling Cites

Chamling references a 2012 genetic study by Tenzin Gayden that found:

  • Newar ancestors have been in Kathmandu Valley for 13,000–17,000 years
  • Newars have three major genetic components:
    1. Tibeto-Burman (largest) — from Kirat ancestry
    2. Dravidian/Munda — from earlier inhabitants
    3. Aryan — from later arrivals (Licchavi period onward)
  • Modern Kirats (Rai, Limbu) show 96-100% East Asian genes

🔗 Oral Traditions Connect East to Kathmandu

The Mundhum (Kirat oral tradition) contains fascinating connections to Kathmandu:

Ancestor Spirit Guidance: When guiding deceased ancestors' spirits, eastern Kirats direct them toward Kathmandu — "your people who look like you, speak like you, are there."
Metal (Datu) References: Ritual mentions of metals connect to Kathmandu Valley's mineral resources.
Rice Cultivation: Eastern Kirats maintain rice-offering rituals despite living where rice doesn't grow — suggesting ancestral memory of Kathmandu's rice paddies.

Based on Chamling's research and our genetic profile, there's a compelling case that our maternal ancestors were among the Kirat population of Kathmandu Valley — possibly the very people who ruled the valley before the Licchavis arrived ~2,000 years ago.
